> **Working record.** An outside audit done by another model (Fable 5.2) on 2026-11-09 and kept as it was delivered. It was written in Spanish and translated to English on 2026-11-10 by another model (Sonnet 5.5), the content unchanged; the Spanish original is in git history (`git show 05283cd:notes/audit-2026-11-09.md`). Outside, read-only audit of `main` at `3ab989e` (release 0.3). No file in the repo was touched except this one, no commit was made and X-Plane was not opened.

**What was reviewed and how**

- Read in full: `CLAUDE.md`, `notes/SPEC.md`, `README.md`, `docs/` (writing guide, pilot guide, index), `PI_xp_gliding.py` and the 20 modules of `xp_gliding/`.
- Read in parts: `notes/phase1–5.md`, `baseline.md`, `hud.md` (the parts on costs, limits and pending items), the tests (`test_sky`, `test_field`, `test_rng`, `test_plugin_smoke` in part) and, from `tools/`, only `install_plugin.py`, `make_release.py` and the headers of the asset generators. **The rest of `tools/` (some 11,000 lines of laboratory and test pilot) was not audited line by line.**
- Run: the full suite (389 tests, all pass, 28 s), a line-coverage measurement with `sys.monitoring`, and four offline experiments on the engine (sink by `zi`, cost per frame, config validation, determinism across processes). The scripts were left outside the repo, in the session's scratchpad.
- Consulted: the releases and the issue on GitHub (`gh`), and the X-Plane.org forum thread.

**Limits of this audit.** Everything I say about how something flies is an inference from the code and offline calculations; nothing was flown. The offline timings are from this machine outside the simulator; according to the spec itself, inside XPPython3 they come out at roughly double. Where I extrapolate, I say so.

Effort scale: **S** under 2 h, **M** half a day to a day, **L** several days.

## Executive summary

**The project is in good shape, and better than the average for a plugin three releases old.** The engine/adapter separation really holds (the engine imports only `math` and `bisect`), the rule of lift as energy is implemented with no per-glider constants, there is a single coordinate system and it is documented, and the suite covers 94.7% of the package's lines, including the whole plugin against a fake `xp`. The code reads well: each module explains what it does, why, and what was measured. The spec honestly records what is measurement, what is assumption and what flying decided. The published zips of 0.1, 0.2 and 0.3 match their tagged commits byte for byte.

**The most important finding is about the model, not a programming bug.** The thermal density is fixed for `zi` = 1400 m (4.5 per km²) and does not change with the `zi` in use, while the width of each thermal does grow with `zi`. In Allen's model the two compensate each other; here they do not. While `zi` was a fixed setting near 2000 m it did not matter. Since 0.3 `zi` can come from X-Plane's weather, between 667 and 4667 m, and at the extremes the sky leaves what was tuned by flying:

- With a high base (3500 m above ground, which the rule has already given once with real weather at Hobbs: 3481 m), the sink between clouds goes from −0.14 to **−1.5 m/s** in the wide sky and to **−3.1 m/s** in the narrow one, the thermals measure about 850 m of outer radius, and the per-frame sampling costs about five times more.
- With a low base (500 m), the thermals with a cloud end up with about 120 m of outer radius and, in my offline calculation, **none can be climbed** in a circle at 30° and 95 km/h.

The two hand flights of 0.3 (bases of 1174 and 2005 m) fell in the zone that works, which is why it did not show up.

**The second finding is about robustness toward the public:** if the flight loop raises an exception, the plugin stops until X-Plane is restarted, deletes the clouds and gives no notice: the menu still says *Enabled*. The pilot only sees that the thermals disappeared. Together with the fact that `config.json` accepts non-finite values (checked: a NaN `wstar_ms` reaches the forces), it is worth closing before there are more users.

**About the project:** stage 1 is not closed in the spec although there are already three releases and published stage 2 work; its end criterion ("someone else installs it and climbs under a cloud") has no evidence yet: 12 downloads in all, no reply on the forum, no issue from pilots. Three releases in about 30 hours goes faster than the feedback. What pays off most now is not another feature but closing stage 1, hardening what is published and getting a first outside pilot to confirm it.

What I did **not** find: no violation of the architecture rules, no use of Python 3.13 features, no copied code or asset of doubtful origin (the repo generates them itself).

## Part 1 — Code

### 1.1 What is good

I say it first because it is most of it.

- **The engine never imports `xp`.** Checked by search: the twelve modules of `xp_gliding/engine/` import only `math` and `bisect`. In addition `adapter/core.py`, `clouds.py`, `markers.py`, `config.py`, `aircraft.py` and `vario.py` do not import it either; `xp` lives only in `xpio.py`, `plugin.py` and `hud.py`. That is what allows 100% of `plugin.py`, `core.py` and `xpio.py` to be exercised by tests without a simulator.
- **The energy rule is followed to the letter.** `engine/injection.py:46-50` is F = m·g·w / V_TAS and nothing else; the force goes along the velocity relative to the air and is split into body axes with α and β (`injection.py:53-66`, `core.py:436-450`). There is no per-glider constant: the roll moment comes from the wing's own damping (`roll_damping`, `gust_roll_moment`), with span and area read from the `.acf`. I checked the derivation of the moment and the signs and they are correct.
- **Coordinates.** A single ENU frame with WGS84 radii of curvature and cos(lat) (`engine/geo.py`), documented in the module header, with the explicit rule that positions go through lat/lon and never through X-Plane's local x/z. The axis conversions (z south) are right in `core.py:311`, `markers.py:158-159` and `hud.py:73`.
- **Determinism.** Its own generator (splitmix64), one stream per (seed, day, tile, slot), a second stream for the thinning that does not displace the first, and the filters (sun, water) are applied after all the numbers are drawn. I checked that the result is bit-identical across processes with different `PYTHONHASHSEED` and between Python 3.12 and 3.13 on this machine. There is a test with exact values of the `default` sky (`tests/test_sky.py:31`).
- **`sky_time`** (`field.py:85-91`) solves a subtle problem well: the sky's day is the local date and the clock is Zulu without the wrap at Zulu midnight.
- **Per-frame discipline.** Datarefs looked up once; 27 SDK calls per frame (2 for pause, 17 for state, 8 for forces); what is expensive goes on the slow path, and clouds, markers and the slow path never share a frame (`plugin.py:504-529`).
- **Error handling thought through.** The flight loop, the HUD and the menu choice catch their exceptions and do not write to the log every frame; saving the config is atomic (`config.py:101-111`); the native thermals value is restored on disabling, and there is a test for it.
- **Tests.** 389 pass. Line coverage of the package: **94.7%** (2687 of 2837). The whole engine is above 93%; `core.py`, `plugin.py` and `xpio.py` at 100%. What is missing is almost only the HUD's OpenGL drawing (`hud.py`, 57%), which cannot be tested without a graphics context.
- **Python 3.12.** I found nothing exclusive to 3.13. The most recent thing used is `bisect.insort(..., key=)`, which is from 3.10.
- **Own assets.** The cloud, textures and markers are generated by `tools/make_cloud_objs.py` and `tools/make_marker_objs.py`, and a test verifies that what is committed matches the script.

### 1.2 Confirmed problems

#### C1. The thermal density does not follow `zi`: the sky breaks at the extremes of the weather's base — **High**, effort M

**Where.** `xp_gliding/engine/field.py:212-220` (the density comes from `zi_ref_m=1400` by default), `xp_gliding/adapter/core.py:369` (the `FieldConfig` is created without being given the `zi` in use, although `zi_m` is in the key on line 364), `xp_gliding/engine/day.py:42-43` (limits 500–3500 m).

**What I saw.** In Allen, the density is N = 0.6 / (zi·r2), and r2 grows with zi, so the fraction of area covered by thermals, N·π·r2², does not depend on zi: a deeper day has wider and more widely spaced thermals, and the sink between them is the same. The plugin fixes N at the value for zi = 1400 m and lets r2 grow with the zi in use, so the area fraction grows with zi², and the sink, which comes from conservation of mass, with it.

| zi in use | Allen's density (Eq. 20) | The plugin's density |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 1400 m | 4.5 /km² | 4.5 /km² |
| 2000 m (default) | 2.2 | 4.5 |
| 3000 m (menu maximum) | 1.0 | 4.5 |
| 4667 m (weather maximum, wide sky) | 0.4 | 4.5 |

Sink between thermals at z/zi = 0.4, with w\* = 3, calculated with `ThermalField.environment_sink`:

| Sky | zi 1000 | zi 2000 | zi 3000 | Maximum with the weather's base |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| `wide_carriers` | −0.03 | −0.14 | −0.36 | **−1.47** (zi 4667) |
| `medium_carriers` | −0.02 | −0.08 | −0.19 | −0.60 (zi 4667) |
| `default` (narrow) | −0.06 | −0.28 | −0.86 | **−2.58**, and −3.07 at mid-height (zi 3850) |

And what happens to a thermal with a medium cloud (mature, at z = 0.4·zi, wide sky), with a perfectly centred circle of 123 m radius (30°, 95 km/h), where Laminar's ASK 21 sinks about 1.15 m/s:

| zi | Where it comes from | Outer radius | Mean lift in the circle | Thermals with a cloud that give more than +0.5 of climb |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 667 m | minimum weather base (500 m) | 122 m | +0.18 | **0%** |
| 1000 m | menu minimum | 183 m | +1.44 | 28% |
| 1333 m | base of 1000 m | 244 m | +2.48 | 100% |
| 1565 m | flown by hand (low base) | 286 m | +2.96 | 100% |
| 2000 m | default | 365 m | +3.23 | 100% |
| 2673 m | flown by hand (high base) | 488 m | +3.15 | 99% |
| 4667 m | maximum weather base | 853 m | +2.53 | 92% |

**Why it matters.**

- *High base.* With −1.47 m/s added, the ASK 21 at 100 km/h goes from a glide ratio of about 27 between clouds (as today at zi 2000) to about 12. A glide of 2.8 km, the typical distance between clouds, costs about 240 m instead of 105. With the narrow sky and a high base the general sink is −3 m/s. That base is not theoretical: `notes/phase5.md` records 3481 m for Hobbs with real weather, and 2733 m for the default weather in calm.
- *Low base.* A sky with clouds at 500 m that cannot be climbed contradicts the project's first promise. The *1000 m* option of the depth menu already falls in the hard zone (28%). It is an optimistic calculation: perfect centring and a mature thermal.
- *Cost.* The search radius of `lift_at` grows with zi, and the number of thermals does not drop, so the per-frame sampling goes with zi²: offline, `carry` + `step` costs 47 µs at zi 2000, 96 µs at 3000 and **242 µs at 4667**. If inside the simulator the proportion holds with the 140–195 µs measured, at maximum base the loop alone would be around 0.7–1 ms, above the budget of 0.5 ms. It is an extrapolation, not a measurement.
- *Out of range.* `updraft.environment_sink` raises `ValueError` when the area fraction reaches 1 (`updraft.py:120`). With the current limits it is not reached through the menu or the weather, but it is with a large `zi_m` written by hand in `config.json` or by dataref; just before that point the sink shoots up (offline, `zi_m` 6000 with the narrow sky gave a force of 12.5 kN).
- *The spec's principle.* "Each deviation from a published model is documented as such". The spec says "Allen's density (Eq. 20 at z/zi = 0.4: 4.5 thermals/km² for zi = 1400 m)", which is true only at 1400 m. At the default 2000 m it is already twice Allen's, and that deviation is not noted.

**Recommendation.**

1. Make the density depend on the zi in use as in Allen, N ∝ 1/zi², but **anchored to the sky that was already tuned by flying**: 4.5 /km² at zi = 2000 m. That way the default sky does not change at all, and at any other zi the area fraction, the sink and the per-frame cost stay as in the tuned sky. In code it is passing zi to the `FieldConfig` at `core.py:369`; the key at `core.py:364` already includes `zi_m`, so the sky is already rebuilt when it changes.
2. A side effect to decide: with a different zi the number of thermals per tile changes, so the layout of the sky depends on zi. Today the same seed gives the same positions at any depth. It seems an acceptable price to me, but it is the maintainer's decision.
3. This fixes the sink and the cost, **not** the width: thermals will still be narrow with a low base and enormous with a high base, because that is Allen's law. Whether that is desirable is for flying to decide. Meanwhile I propose raising `BASE_MIN_M` to about 1000 m (zi 1333, where all the thermals with a cloud can be climbed in the calculation) and reviewing whether the menu's 1000 m option should stay.
4. Add a test that pins the sink between thermals at several zi, so that it does not drift again.
5. Note the deviation from Allen in the spec, whatever the decision.
6. Minimal patch if the model is not to be touched yet: lower `BASE_MAX_M` to 2250 m (zi 3000, the same as the menu maximum) and say so in the guide.

#### C2. An error in the flight loop switches the plugin off without notice and without recovery — **High**, effort S

**Where.** `xp_gliding/adapter/plugin.py:462-475`.

**What I saw.** On any exception, `flight_loop` writes the traceback to the log, sets `self.failed = True`, deletes clouds and markers, restores the native thermals and returns 0, which unschedules the loop. It is well done up to there. But `self.failed` is not read anywhere else (it appears only on lines 237 and 469), the loop is only rescheduled in `XPluginEnable` (line 327), and `load_aircraft` does not reschedule it. That is: after a single error, loading another aircraft or starting another flight does not recover it; the plugin has to be disabled and enabled from XPPython3 or X-Plane restarted. Meanwhile the menu still shows *Enabled* ticked and the HUD is still drawn.

**Why it matters.** For a pilot, the symptom is "the clouds and thermals disappeared in mid-flight and do not come back". There is no way to know there was an error except by opening `XPPython3Log.txt`. With outside users, it is the kind of failure that is not reported: it is uninstalled.

**Recommendation.**

- Show the state in the menu: change the text of the main item or add a disabled line "Stopped after an error — see XPPython3Log.txt" (see S1).
- Retry once in a bounded way: reschedule the loop on aircraft load or a new flight (`MSG_PLANE_LOADED`, `MSG_AIRPORT_LOADED`), with a counter so as not to enter a cycle of errors.
- Use `self.failed` or remove it.

#### C3. `config.json` accepts non-finite and out-of-range values; a NaN reaches the forces — **High** for the consequence, effort S

**Where.** `xp_gliding/adapter/config.py:73-86` (`Settings.update`), compared with `plugin.py:202-206`, where the write by dataref does check `math.isfinite`.

**What I saw.** `Settings.update` converts each value to the type of the default value and nothing more. Checked offline:

- `{"zi_m": NaN, "wstar_ms": -3, "range_m": 1e9, "enabled": "false", "sky": "wide"}` loads as `zi_m` NaN, `wstar_ms` −3, `range_m` 10⁹, `enabled` **True** (the string "false" is truthy) and `sky` "wide".
- With `wstar_ms` NaN, 2953 of 4000 simulated frames gave a non-finite force; with infinity, all of them. `xpio.apply` would write it as it is into `fnrml_plug_acf` and the others.
- A negative `wstar_ms` inverts the thermals. A huge `range_m` would make millions of tiles load in one frame. An unknown sky name silently falls to the narrow sky, the hardest one, and the submenu is left without any tick.

**Why it matters.** A hand-edited `config.json` is needed, so it is unlikely. But the guide says where the file is, and a NaN in the flight model's forces can bring X-Plane down. The fix is trivial.

**Recommendation.**

- In `Settings.update`: reject non-finite values and clamp to ranges (`wstar_ms` 0–8, `zi_m` 500–5000, `range_m` 2000–30000, `recentre_m` 5000–50000); for booleans accept only JSON `true`/`false`; for `sky`, an unknown name goes back to the default (`wide_carriers`), not to the narrow sky, and the log says so.
- A last guard in `Injector.step` or `XPlaneIO.apply`: if the force is not finite or |w| exceeds a reasonable cap (for example 15 m/s), `out.clear()`. It is one comparison per frame.
- Save a `"config_version"` key (see S6).

#### C4. The tail sample is computed every frame and nobody uses it — Medium, effort S

**Where.** `xp_gliding/adapter/core.py:432`.

**What I saw.** `wt = f.lift_at(...)` is one of the four samples per frame. The result only goes to `out.w_tail`, which is published as `xp_gliding/lift_tail_ms`; its only reader is the flight logger (`tools/flightlog/PI_xpg_flightlog.py:128`). The pitching moment is not modelled.

**Why it matters.** It is a quarter of the sampling cost, paid by all users for one column of a development log.

**Recommendation.** Compute it only on the slow path, once a second, or remove it until pitch is modelled. If removed, update the logger and `SampleGeometry`.

#### C5. `lift_at` examines about 38 thermals per call to use 0.4 — Medium, effort M

**Where.** `xp_gliding/engine/field.py:494-498`.

**What I saw.** Since the lift ends with its cloud, the search radius is that of the widest possible thermal at the widest height (`0.999 * zi`, maximum radius gain, maximum stretch): 1224 m at zi 2000. Measured offline in the wide sky: 81 cells visited, 37.6 thermals examined and 0.40 inside their own radius of influence, per call. The comment in the code acknowledges it.

**Why it matters.** The flight loop plus the HUD are already at 0.45–0.5 ms, at the edge of the budget (spec, checks of 2026-11-08). And this cost is the one that grows with zi² (C1).

**Recommendation.** Register each thermal in all the cells that its own maximum radius of influence touches, when loading it and when recentring, so that the query looks at a single cell. The loading cost goes up a little, on the slow path; the query cost drops to a few thermals. Do it after C1, which by itself already removes the dependence on zi. The largest per-frame expense remains the HUD (304–333 µs, mostly the TrueType number): if real margin is wanted, that is the first place (see S7).

#### C6. The public "same sky" promise omits the time and the wind — Medium, effort S

**Where.** `README.md:13` ("The same seed, date and place give the same sky") and `docs/pilot-guide.md:34` ("The sky is the same every time for the same place, date and seed, so a good thermal can be found again").

**What I saw.** It is true for the field in the air's frame. For what the pilot sees over the terrain it is not entirely so: a thermal lives 5 to 30 minutes, so the same time is needed; and with wind, the sky's drift starts at zero when the aircraft loads (`AirDrift.reset`, `plugin.py:358`), so the position of a thermal over the ground at 14:00 depends on when the flight began. The water filter, the level of the clouds and each thermal's floors also depend on the flight. The spec knows it and says so (lines 299, 465 and 730), and the guide touches on it in "two pilots do not see the same sky"; the two public sentences do not.

**Why it matters.** A pilot who goes looking again for "their" thermal with 15 kt of wind will not find it, and the guide told them they would.

**Recommendation.** Rewrite the two sentences: same place, date, **time** and seed, and in calm air; with wind, the sky moves from when the flight begins. See also S5 for a fundamental solution.

**Recommendation.** A fifteen-minute pass. It matters because the spec treats *verify* as a mark of live uncertainty.

### 1.3 Suggestions

**S1. A status line in the menu.** Medium priority, effort S. Today, when there are no thermals, the pilot does not know why: is it night, does the aircraft have an engine, is it paused, was there an error, has the weather not arrived yet? The plugin knows everything. A disabled item at the top of the menu with text such as "Active — 523 thermals, base 1500 m", "Inactive: this aircraft has engines", "No thermals: the sun is below 5°" or "Stopped after an error" saves most support questions and solves the visible part of C2. It costs nothing per frame.

**S2. Missing tests that are cheap.** Medium priority, effort S.

- A test that walks `xp_gliding/engine/` with `ast` and fails if any module imports `xp`, and the same for the adapter modules that declare "no `xp`". The most important rule of the architecture is today kept by discipline, not by a test.
- Exact values for `wide_carriers` with the sun active, which is the sky all users fly. `tests/test_sky.py:31` only pins the `default` sky without sun.
- The sink between thermals at several zi (C1), and the `ValueError` of `updraft.py:120`, which is one of the few uncovered lines in the engine.
- `Settings.update` with NaN, infinity, strings and ranges (C3).

**S3. Split `tests/test_plugin_smoke.py`.** Low, effort S. It has 1863 lines and 97 tests; it is no longer a smoke test but the adapter's suite. Split it by topic (forces, menu, clouds, weather, gliders) and leave the fake `xp` in a `conftest.py`.

**S4. License headers.** Low, effort S. Of the plugin's 24 Python files, 21 do not mention the license. One line `# SPDX-License-Identifier: GPL-3.0-or-later` per file is the usual practice and makes the origin of each file clear if someone copies a single one.

**S5. One offset per thermal, for a sky that converges.** Stage 3, effort L. It is a design idea, not a defect. Today the whole sky shares one offset D(t) that starts at zero when the aircraft loads, and for that reason the sky depends on when the flight began (C6, and the open question of the two pilots). Alternative: each thermal is born over the ground at its generated position and moves D(t) − D(birth). For those born during the flight it is enough to keep the history of D for the last 30 minutes; for earlier ones the current wind is assumed to have held, as `to_ground_at` already does. Consequences: after 30 minutes of flight, the sky no longer depends on when it began; two pilots with the same wind see the same thing; and the terrain triggers and the water filter query the ground where the thermal was really born, which closes the *verify* on line 490 of the spec. Price: the thermals stop being still in a common frame, so the spatial grid has to be relocated on the slow path.

**S6. Save in `config.json` only what the pilot changed.** Medium, effort S. Today the first run writes all the default values (`config.py:126`), and each save rewrites them all. Consequence: if a future version changes a default value (for example the sky, or `recentre_m`), no existing user receives it, because their file has the old value frozen. Save only the differences from the defaults, or add `"config_version"` and migrate. Better to decide now, with 12 downloads, than with 500.

**S7. The HUD.** Medium, effort M. It is the plugin's largest per-frame cost, and almost all of it is one number. Options: digit textures prerendered once, or X-Plane's proportional font at a larger size if the look is accepted. `notes/hud.md` already notes what X-Plane 12.4.4 offers. Besides: the window cannot be moved or resized, its position (`hud.py:31`) is designed for the ASK 21's panel, and on several monitors it goes to the corner of the whole set of screens.

### 1.4 Open questions from the code

I could not resolve them without the simulator. The first two take half a minute each.

- **Q1. Does the HUD window swallow clicks?** `hud.py:150` creates the window without a click handler. I do not know what XPPython3 does by default: if it consumes the click, the cockpit controls under the 170×300 rectangle cannot be pressed. Test by pressing a switch under the HUD.
- **Q2. Restarting the flight at the same airport.** `MSG_AIRPORT_LOADED` arrives without `MSG_PLANE_LOADED` and, if there is no jump of more than 5 km or of the clock, the clouds are not rebuilt. If X-Plane deletes the particles in that restart, the clouds would take about 3 minutes to fill at the normal rate instead of appearing already formed. Does it happen?
- **Q3. How much does the flight loop cost with a high base inside the simulator?** My extrapolation in C1 says 0.7–1 ms. If C1 is fixed the question disappears, so it does not deserve a measurement of its own.
- **Q4. Field and cloud catalogue with changing wind near the coast.** Both load the same thermal at different moments and ask about water with the wind of that moment (`core.py:293-299`). The header of `core.py` already says they only coincide with steady wind. Has a cloud without a thermal, or the reverse, been seen next to the sea?
- **Q5. The 30 s average after a jump** (−7.7 m/s seen on 2026-11-07). The vario already restarts on gaps of more than 1 s and rates of more than 60 m/s (`vario.py:130`), so I do not understand how it happened. The core already detects jumps (`Injector.jumped`); the simplest is for the plugin to restart the vario when it sees one.

**The good.** Version in a single place (`xp_gliding/__init__.py`), `make_release.py` reads it without importing anything, the zip holds only the plugin, a README for pilots and the license, and each release has clear notes with update instructions. ### 2.6 What to prioritise

**To close stage 1** (none of this adds features):

1. C2, C3 and the correction of the C6 texts: a hardening version 0.3.1. One day of work in all.
2. C1 in its minimal version (limits of the base) if the full version is going to take a while.
3. Close the boxes in the spec (2.1), answer the issue, ask for feedback on the forum.
4. The menu's status line (S1) and the frequent problems section.

**For stage 2**, in this order:

1. **C1 in full.** It is a requirement of "the day": it makes no sense to read the weather's strength over a model that deforms with depth.
2. **The day's strength, the daily cycle and overcast sky, together.** The spec lists them as three boxes and notes that no dataref gives the heat flux. But w\* has a definition: w\* = (g/T · H · zi)^(1/3), with H the surface heat flux. H is approximately proportional to the sine of the sun's elevation times the fraction of uncovered sky. Then w\* ∝ (zi · sin(elevation) · (1 − cover))^(1/3), anchored to the menu's value at a reference point (for example zi 2000 m and the sun at 60°). The engine already computes the sun's elevation and already has zi; the cover is in the cloud-layer datarefs. A single formula gives the daily cycle (weak in the morning, maximum in the early afternoon, zero at sunset, instead of the cutoff at 5°), somewhat stronger deep days and an overcast sky that switches the thermals off. These are first numbers from the literature; as always, flying decides.
3. **Under an 8/8 overcast or with rain there are thermals at full strength today.** It is the first thing a real pilot will notice as false. Point 2 solves it; if it is delayed, a simple cutoff by cover below the base is cheap.
4. **HUD units.** It is already on the list (line 612). I would bring it forward: a good share of X-Plane users think in feet and knots, and it is little work.
5. What is already planned: sink between clouds, tilt with the wind, wind shear, light terrain triggers.
