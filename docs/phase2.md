# Phase 2: training markers and cumulus

Two things drawn at the engine's thermals, and what they showed about the sky. [Cumulus](#cumulus) comes first, as it is what the phase is for; [A wider sky under the clouds](#a-wider-sky-under-the-clouds) is an offline candidate, not flown. Training mode draws a marker over each thermal of the engine near the glider, so a pilot can see where the cores are. It is an aid for flying the thermals (test F), and step zero of phase 2's cumulus: the same mechanism (instanced objects placed at thermal positions, created and destroyed with each thermal's life), tried here before the particle clouds. See the roadmap in [SPEC.md](SPEC.md).

**Status (2026-11-03):** working in X-Plane 12.4.3 under Vulkan with the Laminar ASK 21, flown by the test pilot. Positions match X-Plane's own coordinates within 0.2 m, and no drawing or CPU cost can be told from the noise at 20 markers. Juan Luis has watched them in flight but not flown with them: how they read from the seat is for test F.

## Cumulus

A particle cloud over the thermals that carry one, on by default: menu *Plugins → xp-gliding → Cumulus clouds*, command `xp_gliding/clouds/toggle`, `clouds_enabled` in `config.json` and the writable dataref `xp_gliding/clouds_enabled`. Read-only under `xp_gliding/clouds/`: `count`, `created`, `destroyed`, `rate_changes`, `tiles_missing`, `update_us`, `update_us_max`, `plan_us`, `nearest_m`.

**Status (2026-11-04):** working in X-Plane 12.4.3 under Vulkan, flown by the test pilot. It looks like a cumulus sky from far and from close, with no cost that can be told from the noise. The lift is under the clouds; how well the Laminar ASK 21 can climb in it is below. Juan Luis has seen a first cloud in flight, but has not yet flown under a full sky. Not checked yet: see [Still open](#still-open).

### What decides a cloud

`xp_gliding/engine/clouds.py`, pure and tested offline. A thermal either carries a cloud or it does not, from its own numbers, so the sky is the same for everyone with the same seed, date and place: mass flux `wgain · rgain²` of at least 2.0, and a life of at least 12 min. That is about 3% of the thermals, 0.12 clouds per km², one every ~2.8 km, about 200 within the 20 km they are drawn. The density is a first choice by eye and from the 1.4 zi spacing of convective cells.

| Moment in the thermal's life | Cloud |
| --- | --- |
| 10% | It starts forming: particles at the steady rate, full after one particle life |
| 10% to 70% | Fed steadily, with a size that follows `wgain` × `rgain` (`rgain` alone [until 2026-11-05](phase3.md#first-flight-by-hand)) |
| 70%, when the thermal starts to decay | Feeding stops. It fades as its particles die, 144 to 216 s later |
| 100% | At the shortest life, the fade ends just as the thermal does |

A cloud that comes into range, or the first sky of a flight, should already be as old as it is, so it emits fast for up to 9 s, rests, and then goes on at the steady rate. Beyond 8 km it uses 30% of the particles, and the rest arrive as the glider approaches. Leaving the 21 km limit stops the feeding, so no cloud vanishes in sight.

**Height.** The base is the top of the engine's lift (z/zi = 1/1.1, or the sky's `cloud_base_zzi`) over the ground under the glider when the cloud is created. An emitted particle stays where it is, so a cloud cannot follow the terrain, and real bases are level anyway. So over rough ground neighbouring clouds can have different bases, and the same cloud can have a different base for another pilot (see [Known limits](#known-limits)).

**How it runs** (`xp_gliding/adapter/clouds.py`, SDK calls in `xpio.SkyObjects`). A few times a second while filling, and every 5 s after that, or sooner when a fast emission must end. It never sets positions except when a cloud is created or its rate changes: the instances follow X-Plane's local origin with `instanceSetAutoShift`. The assets are `xp_gliding/assets/clouds/`, generated by `tools/make_cloud_objs.py`; none comes from another plugin.

- **Instances should be created before the flight model:** X-Plane logs that creating them in a post-flight-loop callback is discouraged. The plugin's loop runs before it. The lab's did not.
- **A puff cut by its texture cell** shows as a straight line from close up. Puffs now fade out inside their cell (tested).

The plugin's own work happens in single frames, which a smoothed `frame_us` hides. In the last flights:

| Work | Cost | How often |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Loading 2 tiles | ~500 µs (3 tiles: 560–850) | 3 times a second, for about 3 min after the start, then rarely |

The budget is 500 µs per frame, so planning passes it once every few seconds. It is mostly Python over the catalog, and it could spread over several frames if that ever mattered. The first version reached 10.7 ms once, presumably destroying many clouds of a dropped tile in one frame; they now fade instead, and that peak has not come back.

The strongest thing shown here is the distance table above: the lift is under the clouds. Whether they are good enough to climb in comes down to a hand flight, and to a better orbit from the test pilot.

### What this says about the sky

- **The glider does not reach the clouds.** Allen's lift falls to nothing at the clouds' base, and the offline table has almost no climb above ~0.7 zi, so a glider tops out about 400 m below the base. In real life the best lift is often right under the cloud. That is a question for the sky's tuning, which is still to decide (see the SPEC's open questions).
- **The climb is thin with this glider.** See above; it is the same finding as in phase 1.
- **A better orbit first.** Tuning the sky against this test pilot's climbs would be tuning against its swing.

Where the good air is, and how wide, led to a candidate: see [A wider sky under the clouds](#a-wider-sky-under-the-clouds).

## A wider sky under the clouds

**Status (2026-11-05): measured offline, and one climb flown by hand.** The default sky has not changed. A named sky, `wide_carriers`, holds a candidate. Its numbers are a first guess to judge by flying, and no measured reference in this repository supports them: everything below says what the candidate does to our own sky, not that it is right.

### Why

Juan Luis flew under a full sky by hand for the first time on 2026-11-04, over Santiago, and noticed that the thermals did not seem to be right under the clouds. Measured offline at the same place (`tools/engine_plots/clouds_vs_lift.py`), he was right, and the cause was the design, not a misplacement. A cloud's centre has good lift, but only about 12% of the marked columns have their own cloud, about 22% are under any cloud, the nearest cloud is about 1.2 km away, and only about 30% of the air at +2 m/s or more is under a cloud. A cloud's body is about 900 m wide over lift about 270 m wide.

His idea: thermals with a cloud about twice as wide and as strong as they are, with few blue ones. His condition: the sky is to be judged by real thermals and how it flies, not tuned to make up for Laminar's weak ASK 21. So the circle's mean lift below says nothing about a glider, and only the climb uses the Laminar ASK 21's sink from test A.

### What changed in the engine

`SkyProfile` in `xp_gliding/engine/field.py`, five numbers, all 1.0 by default. `FieldConfig(sky=...)` takes one, `SKIES` names them. The draw is untouched, so a profile moves nothing: the same stream gives the same thermals in the same places, and the clouds are the ones of today's sky. Thinning uses a second random stream with its own key, so the first is the same with or without it. A test pins values of the sky from before the change: `lift_at`, the sink and a tile are identical to the last digit.

| Number | Default | `wide_carriers` | What it does |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| `carrier_stretch` | 1 | 2 | A thermal that carries a cloud is this much wider, shape and all: its lift at r is Allen's at r / stretch |
| `carrier_gain` | 1 | 1 | Its strength. The stretch keeps the peak, so it stays at 1 |
| `blue_keep` | 1 | 0.15 | The share of the other thermals that exist. The rest are dropped |
| `blue_gain` | 1 | 0.6 | The strength of those that remain |
| `blue_stretch` | 1 | 0.75 | Their width |
| `cloud_base_zzi` | 1/1.1 | 0.75 | The clouds' base as z/zi, also the HUD's `TOP` and the top of the markers' columns. It changes no lift |

The environment sink is not a number to pick. It follows from conservation of mass (`mean_flux_gain`, closed form, compared with a large sample in a test).

**How to choose it.** In the plugin, add `"sky": "wide_carriers"` to `Output/xp-gliding/config.json` and restart; a config without the key, or with a name it does not know, flies the default sky. In the tools, `--sky wide_carriers`, and `--set carrier_stretch=1.75` to try other numbers. The test pilot's missions read the plugin's sky from the dataref `xp_gliding/sky_index`, so their oracle sees the same lift (not flown yet); `tools/hudlab/check_aid.py` takes `--sky`.

**Two deviations from Allen, on record as the others are:**

- **The stretch, instead of a larger `rgain`.** Allen's Eq. 13 raises the core ratio r1/r2 with r2, so doubling r2 gives a flat top with a steep edge and a peak 20% lower, and it takes `wgain` x1.25 to get the peak back. Stretching keeps Allen's shape and his mean updraft at every height, so the strength stays as it is, and it needs nothing new in `updraft.py`. Flux goes up by stretch squared, which the sink accounts for.
- **Far fewer thermals than Allen's Eq. 20.** About 0.65 blue ones per km² instead of about 4.4. His density comes from measured spacing at z/zi = 0.4. In our field each of those thermals has a core worth going to (median +2.1 to +2.4 m/s at 500-800 m AGL), which is why most of the good air had no cloud, and those are what is removed. There is no way to keep that density and widen: stretching every thermal x2 gives a sink of −1.9 to −4.3 m/s and a mean of the air of −0.6 to −2.0 (the area they cover is no longer small, `--set carrier_stretch=2 --set blue_stretch=2`).

Other seeds agree (`cloud_climb.py`, 31°, 500 and 800 m AGL): seed 2, +2.3 and +2.0; seed 3, +2.6 and +2.2; every cloud above 0 in both, and at least 90% above +1. The climbs use the turn model of `circle_climb.py`, which was already optimistic at 45° (see [above](#is-the-lift-under-the-clouds)).

**What it does to circling.** Today a tight turn is best, because the core is small: at 800 m the 45° circle gives +1.6 and the 31° one +0.7. In the candidate that turns round: +2.3 at 31° against +2.2 at 45°. And it forgives: 60 m off centre costs about 0.2-0.3 m/s at 31°, against 0.3 m/s from a climb of +0.5-0.7 today, and 1 m/s at 45°.

**Overlap.** It does not become a problem. With clouds at the same places, the next carrier is 2 half-peak radii (summed) away at the median; 12-18% are closer than that, so some pairs merge. They are few, and the air stays below today's worst, since the blue thermals that today add to each other are gone: air at +2 made by two or more thermals drops from 16-48% to 6-15%.

**Mass balance.** The sink is about half of today's (−0.12 against −0.24 at 550 m AGL), because the flux of the sky is about half (0.57 against 1.04 thermals of mean size per thermal drawn): the carriers, about 5% of the thermals drawn, hold 92% of it. So gliding between clouds gets easier than today. Whether that is too easy is for flying to say. A stronger sink cannot simply be chosen: to sink 0.6 m/s, say, the carriers would have to carry about four times their flux, which means four times as many clouds or twice as wide again.

**The top of the climb.** Width raises it by about 200 m at 31° and 50 m at 45°. What stops the climb is Allen's mean updraft, which dies toward z/zi = 1/1.1 whatever the width, and a base there can never be reached: 370-420 m short in the Laminar ASK 21. So the candidate lowers the base to z/zi = 0.75, 1500 m AGL, where a median carrier has about +1.3 m/s left and the climb at 31° runs out (−0.1 m/s; +0.2 at 1400 m), and the lift stays as it was. That figure comes from this glider's sink, so a better glider will go on climbing into the cloud, as it could in real life. It does not make the lift under a cloud the best of the climb, which is often the case in real life. For that the lift's shape would have to change. In a scratch calculation (not in the engine), a median carrier with a layer 25% deeper than zi, stretched x1.6 to keep its width, climbs to within 40 m of an 1818 m base at 31°; that is the same ratio of base to zi, with more lift near the top. Nothing says yet that lift should be like that.

**What it may cost.** The centring cues get weaker, since the wide core is flat. On a 106 m circle, the first harmonic of the lift with the centre 60 m off is 0.43 m/s against 1.88 today at 550 m AGL, and at 1000 m 0.17 against 1.31; the HUD's centring aid was reliable from about 60 m off centre in today's sky ([hud.md](hud.md)). The steepest difference across a 17 m span halves, from 0.75 to 0.37 m/s, so the roll on entering a thermal will be softer. Neither has been flown. Nothing was changed in the HUD.

| Passes at +1 m/s or more: per km, median width | 0.37-0.48, 140 m | 0.10-0.13, 240-360 m | 0.11-0.15, 280-480 m |

At x1.5 a steeper turn pays again at 500 m, as the handbook says, and every cloud still gives more than +1 at 31° at these heights. Not flown yet.

### First flight by hand

The flight log now has a `speedbrake_ratio` column.
- **High up, he found almost no thermals.** At about 1540 m AGL, with clouds still above him, the nearest cores gave +0.1 to +0.6 m/s. That is what the model says, and it is why the base came down: the clouds promised lift where there was none.
- **The HUD's `TOP` read as wrong to him.** It is the height left up to the top, so it grows as the glider sinks: +1748 m at 70 m AGL. It is right, but the label does not say so.

**The first climb** (2026-11-05, base at 1500 m, airbrakes closed, `flightlog_20261105_001735.csv`). Under a formed cloud, a carrier close to the median (`wgain` 1.39, `rgain` 1.48) at full strength:

The offline tables give +2.7 and +2.3 at 31° and +2.5 and +2.2 at 45°, at 500 and 800 m. The sink is within 0.04 m/s of the turn model (1.47), so here the offline climb can be trusted. ### What remains unknown

- **Most of it in flight.** One climb has been flown. Whether the sky feels right is test F.
- **Whether x2 is the right width,** and the rest of the numbers. They are a first guess.
- **Other days.** Measured at w* 3, zi 2000 m. The stretch scales with Allen's radius, so it should follow other days, but only this one was looked at.
- **Under a cloud, away from its centre.** A cloud's body is about 900 m wide over lift about 530 m wide at +1 for the median carrier at 550 m AGL, up from 270. A pilot aiming at the visual edge still misses; narrower clouds are a choice for after flying. The body has not changed, because it was chosen by eye.
- **Ring sizes.** The largest ring is 112 m and a carrier's core is now 101 m at the median and up to 228 m, so rings of wide carriers stop growing. The column and the colour are right. New objects would be needed, if it is worth it.
- **Carriers with no cloud.** A carrier is alive before its cloud forms and after it has faded, so some marked columns without a cloud can be those. Their life cycle has not changed.

## Training markers

### What it draws

Off by default. For each marked thermal:

- **A ring at the glider's height,** as wide as the thermal's core radius r1 at that height (with its `rgain` and life-cycle radius factor). It is a vertical band 6 m tall, because a flat ring at eye level would be edge-on.
- **A thin column** (8 m wide) from the ground to the top of the lift (z/zi = 1/1.1), to spot the thermal from far.
- **Colour by core lift at the glider's height:**

| Colour | Core lift (m/s) | For the ASK 21 (sinks ~2 m/s circling) |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Pale | below 2 | Cannot climb, it only slows the sink |
| Yellow | 2 to 3 | Barely climbs |
| Orange | 3 to 4 | Good |
| Red | 4 or more | Strong |

**Which thermals** (`xp_gliding/engine/markers.py`, pure and tested offline). The sky is dense: with the defaults about 90 thermals are alive within 3 km and 370 within 5 km. So:

- **Range 3 km:** about 2 minutes of flight at 90 km/h, ~100 m of height at the ASK 21's glide. A 40 m ring still spans 1.5° there.
- **At least 1.5 m/s at the core,** at the glider's height.
- **At most 20,** the best by core lift minus a small cost of distance (0.5 m/s for 2.5 km, the same trade as the oracle in `fly_phase1.py`).

A marker already shown stays with a margin (3.3 km, 1.2 m/s) and gets a ranking bonus, and one within 500 m of the glider cannot be ranked out, so the thermal being circled keeps its marker until its core falls under 1.2 m/s (flown, see [Birth, death and popping](#birth-death-and-popping)). The core is the thermal's own lift, without its neighbours, so where thermals overlap the air is stronger than the colour says.

**Controls.** Menu *Plugins → xp-gliding → Training markers*, command `xp_gliding/markers/toggle`, `markers_enabled` in `Output/xp-gliding/config.json`, and the writable dataref `xp_gliding/markers_enabled`. Read-only under `xp_gliding/markers/`: `count`, `created`, `destroyed`, `swapped`, `update_us`, `update_us_max`, `select_us` and the `probe_*` values of the marker nearest the glider; `light` is writable.

**How it runs** (`xp_gliding/adapter/markers.py`, SDK calls in `xpio.SkyObjects`). Never per frame. About once a second it sets every instance's position, half a second away from the adapter's other slow work so the two never share a frame. Choosing the thermals runs every 5 s. The objects are `xp_gliding/assets/markers/`, generated by `tools/make_marker_objs.py`; none comes from another plugin.

### Results

Flown 2026-11-03 over KHOB, w* 3 m/s, zi 2000 m AGL, seed 1, 2 November at noon, calm air, with `tools/testpilot/fly_markers.py`.

#### Position and height

Checked at the moment of a marker update, every 5 s through 7 minutes of orbits: the distance from the glider to the nearest marker in the engine's frame against the same distance in X-Plane's local coordinates (the aircraft's `local_x/z` against the marker's `worldToLocal`).

| Check | Median | Worst |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Distance in X-Plane minus distance in the engine (m) | 0.03 | 0.19 |
| Height of the ring minus height of the glider (m) | 0.59 | 2.64 |

The horizontal agreement settles the coordinates: lat/lon through `worldToLocal` and the engine's ENU frame agree at these distances. The height difference fits inside the 6 m band; its cause was not looked for (Earth's curvature gives only 0.3 m at 2 km).

A first version of this check read the values from outside the plugin at different moments and showed up to 30 m, which is the glider's speed times the update period. That is why the plugin now takes both at once.

#### A marked core is where the lift is

Engine lift at the glider against the distance to the nearest thermal's centre, 14 minutes of orbits in the same conditions (45 000 frames; the distance is refreshed once a second):

| Distance (m) | Lift, median (m/s) | p10 | p90 |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 0–25 | +5.71 | +4.04 | +7.05 |
| 25–50 | +4.90 | +0.70 | +6.97 |
| 50–75 | +3.22 | +0.51 | +5.36 |
| 75–100 | +2.27 | +0.39 | +3.98 |
| 100–150 | +0.74 | −0.23 | +2.39 |
| 150–200 | −0.15 | −0.28 | +0.81 |
| 200–300 | −0.28 | −0.34 | −0.26 |
| 300–500 | −0.27 | −0.28 | −0.27 |

The climb itself agrees with that lift as in phase 1. The measured netto read 0.08 to 0.34 m/s above the engine's lift in this flight (bank within 1° of the reference circle, speed 5–7 km/h off), and 0.31 and 0.53 m/s above it in the 7-minute one (7–9° and 14–17 km/h off): the residual of the turn model explained in [phase1.md](phase1.md#thermals-of-the-engine).

The median of +5.7 m/s within 25 m is higher than the strongest marker's own core nearby (offline: up to 5.2 m/s at these heights). The likely reason is that the oracle goes for the best circle, which favours thermals overlapping another; that was not checked in these flights. For the colours it matters little: offline, for the 20 marked thermals, the lift with neighbours equals the marker's core at the median, and is up to 2.5 m/s higher for the few that overlap.

#### Cost

Straight flight, markers off and on in alternating blocks of 42 s, 60 fps cap. `frame_us` is the plugin's own smoothed cost per frame, with the field sampled. The GPU time is X-Plane's own estimate, which DataRefs.txt calls "for entertainment value only".

| Markers | Shown | frame_us, median | frame_us, p99 | fps | GPU (ms) | Worst frame (ms) |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| off | 0 | 119 | 145 | 59.8 | 5.78 | 50.3 |
| on | 20 | 116 | 140 | 60.0 | 5.70 | 50.3 |
| off | 0 | 117 | 148 | 59.9 | 5.23 | 50.3 |
| on | 20 | 135 | 168 | 59.8 | 5.20 | 50.3 |
| off | 0 | 161 | 227 | 59.8 | 5.41 | 50.3 |
| on | 20 | 123 | 147 | 59.6 | 4.61 | 50.3 |

**Reading.** The block-to-block noise is larger than any effect of the markers, in the plugin and in the GPU estimate. The 60 fps cap would hide a small drawing cost in the frame rate, and the GPU estimate is the only thing that would show it. Nor does the worst frame change (50.3 ms in every block, with or without markers, so it is not theirs).

The markers' own work happens in single frames, which a smoothed `frame_us` hides, so it is measured apart:

| Work | Cost | How often |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Moving 40 instances (20 `worldToLocal`, 40 `instanceSetPosition`) | ~35 µs | Every second |
| Choosing the thermals, and any instance changes | 440–710 µs | Every 5 s |
| Creating the first 40 instances | 1.1–1.3 ms | Once, when switched on |

The choice can pass the 500 µs budget for a frame, once every 5 s. It is mostly Python over ~90 thermals, and it could spread over several frames if that ever mattered.

**Loading objects is what hurts.** `xp.loadObject` took ~5 ms per object, and 57 ms for the first batch with its textures, inside a frame. Hence `xp.loadObjectAsync` for all 36 objects when first switched on: no frame is blocked, and the markers appeared within 4 s of the command.

#### Birth, death and popping

A marker appears and goes as a whole, since instances have no fade, but every case seen has a cause. Offline, for 10 minutes (`select_markers` every 5 s, same field):

| Flight | Created per minute | Variant changes per minute | Median time shown (s) | Why markers went |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Straight at 25 m/s from 1000 m AGL, sinking 1 m/s | 10.7 | 3.7 | 115 | 53 out of range, 34 ranked out |
| Circling in place at 800 m, climbing 1 m/s | 4.3 | 6.8 | 125 | 22 ranked out, 1 faded |
| Circling in place at 1000 m, level | 4.3 | 3.2 | 170 | 22 ranked out, 1 faded |

In flight: 140 created and 67 variant changes in 14 minutes of orbits, the count holding at 20. With so many thermals in reach, the limit of 20 decides most departures: a strong new thermal displaces a weaker one that is still alive, somewhere in the distance. Few reach the point of fading while shown. Near the glider that can no longer happen (see the protection above). That protection came from this table.

**The thermal being circled keeps its marker** (flown 2026-11-04, `fly_markers.py keep`, plugin at `2d9f2bc`, same air and start as above, 1287 m AGL). The test pilot circled, for 8 minutes at 84 m radius, a marked thermal 486 m from the start, with a 1.9 m/s core and 336 s left to live. Every 5 s the mission asked whether the nearest marker was that thermal, by distance and core.

| Time from the start (s) | What happened |
| --- | --- |
| 0–204 | The nearest marker was the target at all 41 checks; its core went from 1.9 up to 2.2 and back down to about 1.2 m/s |
| 158 | Without the protection it would have left here: a replay of the selection along the flown track, with `protect_m = 0` |
| 204–209 | The marker went, as the core fell through the 1.2 m/s limit for a shown marker |
| 335 | The thermal's life ended |

So the protection did act in this flight, and gave the marker 46 s more. The marker then left on the lift limit, with the thermal still alive but too weak to help: under 1.2 m/s against the ASK 21's ~2 m/s of sink. The 158 s comes from the replay, not from a flight without the protection, and the replay chooses at the mission's 5 s checks rather than at the plugin's own moments. It gave 204 s with the protection, the same as the flight.

It does not act often. An offline search from this start found no case in 270 (thermals within 2.5 km, heights from 600 to 1400 m, level or at 1 m/s up or down, circling at the thermal's centre), so whether it fires depends on the height and the moment. Being close already helps a thermal's rank, since distance has a cost.

### What X-Plane taught (for the cumulus)

Found by trying, in X-Plane 12.4.3 under Vulkan:

- **Instancing from Python works as documented,** and it is cheap: a few µs per call. Instances are not drawn until their first `instanceSetPosition`. Destroying and creating in the same flight loop shows no gap.
- **Small textures are not safe.** A 32×16 palette showed one mixed colour on every object, with no message in the log. At 256×128 the colours were right, and 512×256 is what ships (1 KB as PNG). Presumably X-Plane reduces textures and a small one ends up as its average.
- **`ATTR_light_level` with its optional brightness** gave a colour that does not depend on the sun. Without it, changing the dataref had no visible effect in daylight and the shaded side of a ring went dark. That change also removed `GLOBAL_no_shadow` and `GLOBAL_luminance`, so the brightness is the likely cause, not an isolated one.
- **Alpha.** With plain blending the palette's alpha had no visible effect. With `BLEND_GLASS` far columns look somewhat translucent, while near ones still look almost solid. For a true faint look it needs more work, and the emissive part probably dominates.
- **Do not load objects synchronously,** and the docs warn (XPD-16090) that loading an object with an emitter in `XPluginStart` or `XPluginEnable` can crash the sim. Both point the particle clouds at `loadObjectAsync` from the flight loop.
- **`instanceSetAutoShift`** exists, and suits clouds better than rings: a cloud does not move every second, so with auto-shift it would need no position updates. The markers do not use it, because they are repositioned each second anyway. For up to a second after X-Plane shifts its local origin, a marker would be out of place; no shift happened in these flights.

An object with a particle emitter and its cost were then settled by the [cumulus](#cumulus). **Not settled:** terrain under each thermal.

## Known limits

- **Heights of clouds use the ground under the glider** when each is created, which depends on the glider's path (found in review, 2026-11-04). It does not matter over flat ground like KHOB's; in mountains two pilots see the same cloud at different heights. It wants the terrain under each thermal, as the lift does.
- **Rings at eye level read as a bar, not a circle,** since they are at the glider's height by definition. Seen from above they are clearly circles. Lowering them 20–30 m, or adding a faint ring below, would show an ellipse from the cockpit. To decide after test F.
- **Rings move once a second,** so they step up to ~4 m in a strong climb. To judge in test F.
- **The ring is the core r1, not where to circle.** Half the lift is reached at about 0.67 r2, ~2.3 r1, and an ASK 21 at 45° circles outside the ring.
- **Heights use the ground under the glider,** as the engine's lift does (no terrain under each thermal yet). Over rough ground the column's top moves with the terrain below the glider.
- **While paused,** switching on shows nothing until unpaused. Switching off works at once.
- **Installing from another branch** removes the markers, as `install_plugin.py` replaces the whole package.

## Found in passing

- **The sky changed at zulu midnight.** The field's clock was zulu time, which wraps at 17:00 local at KHOB, while its date is the local one, so every thermal and cloud would change at once in the late afternoon. The field now uses `field.sky_time`, zulu time without that wrap (found in review, 2026-11-04, and tested, not flown). Flights at noon at KHOB keep exactly the same sky.
- **Another crash at the main menu** (2026-11-04, 17:12), "Location undetermined", about 5 s after loading the navigation data, before any flight or cloud: the same kind as the 20:59 one below. The next start took 227 s, and Juan Luis saw it through.
- **X-Plane's Safe Mode dialog** comes up by itself on the start after some crashes (Juan Luis). A restart then looks hung until someone answers "No Thanks". So the 116 s and 225 s starts of this day may be that rather than MOZA, and the ~3.5 min blamed on MOZA is not settled.
- **One crash at the main menu** (2026-11-03, about 20:59), "Location undetermined", with the markers off and no flight requested, on a restart with the wait after the web API shortened to 30 s. The cause is unknown. The default 60 s wait went on to run without problems; one run proves little.
- **`pilots_head_psi` had no effect** when written from the test pilot, in the cockpit view. X-Camera is installed and may be holding that view; not checked. The missions switch between cockpit and outside views for their screenshots instead.
